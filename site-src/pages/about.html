The competition

What is Baja SAE?

Baja SAE is an international collegiate design competition organized by SAE International (bajasae.net). Student teams design, manufacture and race a single-seat, all-terrain vehicle that has to withstand rough terrain, jumps, rocks and mud, and complete a multi-hour endurance race.

Every team uses the engine specified by the rules (currently a Kohler CH440, 14 hp stock but restricted to 10 hp by a restrictor plate) and meets the same safety and design requirements. Differences in performance come down to vehicle design, weight, reliability and team execution, not the powertrain.

Teams are also judged on cost, design justification and a sales presentation, all evaluated by practising engineers from industry. It's a head-to-head test of engineering judgement, manufacturing quality and project management.

02

Competition format

Judged three ways

A Baja SAE event scores the whole program, from the engineering report and cost sheet to how the car holds up after hours of abuse.

01

Static & business

Technical inspection, design judging, a cost report and a sales presentation, defended in front of industry judges.

02

Dynamic events

Acceleration, hill climb or traction, manoeuvrability and suspension events under demanding off-road conditions.

03

Endurance

A four-hour wheel-to-wheel race where reliability and pit-crew coordination count as much as outright speed.

The team

Who we are

Rough Rider Racing (RRR) is the University of Ottawa's Baja SAE team and one of the Faculty of Engineering's competitive design teams, with members from mechanical, electrical, software and computer engineering and other programs.

  • Design, manufacture and race a competitive Baja SAE vehicle that complies with the current rules.
  • Give students practical experience in design, fabrication, testing and project management.
  • Represent the University of Ottawa at competitions across North America and build a program that lasts for future students.

The process

How we work

The program runs on a one-year vehicle cycle: every season the team designs, builds and races a brand-new car. Starting fresh each year raises the design challenge and pushes members toward new solutions instead of carrying old ones forward.

The team is organized into four technical subteams (Chassis, Suspension, Drivetrain and Electrical) and an Administration group responsible for finance, sponsorship, outreach and logistics.
